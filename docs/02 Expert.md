# 02 Expert

Technical position → no management counterpart

Competent individual contributor

> An Expert has moved beyond the learning and onboarding phase. They solve standard problems independently within their domain, take ownership of their work and its outcomes, collaborate actively with their team, and build credibility through consistent, reliable contributions. They don't yet design complex systems or drive larger initiatives, they deliver solid, well-understood work with growing confidence and autonomy.

_Boundaries: [[junior-vs-expert|Junior → Expert]] · [[expert-vs-senior-l1|Expert → Senior L1]]._

## Technical Mastery
**tldr;** An Expert solves standard problems independently in their domain, writes clean and maintainable code, debugs effectively within known systems, and is building depth in their area of expertise.

* **Independent problem-solving**: can solve standard problems within their domain without needing step-by-step guidance — a ticket inside one Roomle surface (a Vue component in roomle-ui, a RAPI endpoint, a fix inside the embedding library, an iOS view) taken from spec to merged PR. Knows when they are stuck and asks the right questions to get unblocked.

* **Clean code**: writes readable, maintainable code that follows established team patterns and conventions. Code reviews rarely surface fundamental issues mostly stylistic or minor improvements.

* **Debugging**: can debug issues within systems they know. Uses systematic approaches (logs, breakpoints, reading stack traces) rather than trial and error. Knows when a bug is within their ability to fix and when to escalate.

* **Domain knowledge**: developing solid depth in their primary technology area. Understands the tools, frameworks, and libraries they work with — not just at surface level, but how they fit together.

* **Roomle vocabulary and Roomle Script literacy**: knows what a static item, configurable item, and configuration are, and can read Roomle Script well enough to *triage* whether a bug is content (a Roomle Script issue from Content Service) or code. Writing production Roomle Script is not expected outside Core / Content Service. (See [[engineering-context#Domain vocabulary|Engineering Context]].)

* **Testing**: writes meaningful tests for their code. Understands the purpose of different testing layers (unit, integration, e2e) and applies them appropriately.

* **Applying patterns**: recognizes and applies standard design patterns and architectural idioms used in the codebase. Doesn't yet invent new approaches, but uses existing ones correctly and consistently.

* **Quality awareness**: pays attention to edge cases, error handling, and input validation in their own code. Reviews their own work before submitting PRs.

## Learning
**tldr;** An Expert actively grows their knowledge within their domain, seeks feedback to improve, and keeps up with developments in their primary technology area.

* **Grows within domain**: actively deepens knowledge in their primary area. Reads documentation, explores APIs, and experiments with features they haven't used before.
* **Seeks feedback**: actively asks for feedback on their work (code reviews, architecture questions, approach discussions) and incorporates it into future work.
* **Stays current**: follows developments in their technology area, new versions, deprecations, best practices. Doesn't need to be told to update their knowledge.
* **Learns from the codebase**: uses existing code as a learning resource. Reads other people's code to understand patterns, conventions, and architectural decisions.
* **Structured growth**: has a sense of what they don't know and is working to close those gaps. Doesn't just wait for tasks to teach them, seeks out learning opportunities within their daily work.

## Responsibility and Ownership
**tldr;** An Expert takes ownership of their assigned work, delivers reliably, accepts accountability for their output, and handles the full cycle of their tasks from implementation to completion.

* **Owns their work**: takes full responsibility for the tasks assigned to them, from understanding the requirements, through implementation, to testing and deployment. Doesn't leave loose ends for others to clean up.
* **Accepts accountability**: when something goes wrong in their area of work, they acknowledge it, learn from it, and fix it. Doesn't deflect or make excuses.
* **Reliable delivery**: completes work within agreed timelines. Communicates early when something will take longer than expected. Doesn't let tasks silently slip.
* **Follows through**: sees tasks through to completion. Doesn't consider work done just because the code is merged, ensures it works in production, documentation is updated, and stakeholders are informed.
* **Works independently**: can be trusted with well-scoped tasks and will deliver without needing constant check-ins. Knows when to ask for help vs. when to figure it out themselves.
* **Takes initiative within scope**: when they notice small improvements (a missing test, a misleading variable name, a confusing comment), they fix them on the go rather than ignoring them.

## Collaboration and Communication
**tldr;** An Expert actively collaborates with team members, communicates clearly about their work, participates constructively in discussions, and contributes to a positive team culture.

* **Active collaboration**: doesn't work in isolation. Actively coordinates with team members when tasks overlap. Reaches out proactively when their work might affect others.
* **Clear communication**: communicates the status of their work clearly and regularly, in standups, in tickets, in PRs. Others never have to wonder what they're working on or whether something is blocked.
* **Constructive participation**: contributes to team discussions (planning, retros, tech discussions). Shares their perspective and asks questions that help the team make better decisions.
* **Helpful in reviews**: gives thoughtful code reviews to others. Points out issues clearly and constructively. Suggests improvements rather than just flagging problems.
* **Receptive to feedback**: takes feedback well, both in code reviews and in personal interactions. Doesn't get defensive; uses feedback as an opportunity to grow.
* **Team awareness**: is aware of what others are working on. Offers help when a colleague is stuck or overloaded. Doesn't tunnel-vision on their own tickets.

## Influence
**tldr;** An Expert builds credibility through consistent, reliable work, contributes actively to projects and team discussions, and is becoming a trusted member of the team.

* **Builds credibility**: earns the trust of their team through consistent delivery, quality work, and professionalism. Others know they can rely on them.
* **Contributes to projects**: actively contributes meaningful work to team projects — not just assigned tickets, but useful additions they identify themselves.
* **Shares knowledge**: shares what they learn with the team — whether in code reviews, informal discussions, or documentation. Doesn't hoard knowledge.
* **Growing presence**: is becoming a known and valued voice in team discussions. Their opinions carry increasing weight as they demonstrate competence.
* **Professional behavior**: is dependable, punctual, and respectful. Communicates professionally in all contexts (PRs, chat, meetings).
* **Positive contributor**: contributes to a good team atmosphere. Approaches challenges with a can-do attitude and doesn't create unnecessary friction.
