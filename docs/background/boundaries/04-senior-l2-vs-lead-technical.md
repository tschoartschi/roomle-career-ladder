# 04 Senior L2 vs Lead (Technical Track) — How We Draw the Line

The transition from Senior L2 (technical) to Lead (technical) is the journey from "multiplier within a team" to "shaper of how engineering works org-wide". It is a step that depends on both demonstrated capability and an available org-shaping scope; in a 30-person company that scope is limited.

The cleanest way to draw the line is on **scope and time horizon**:

| | Senior L2 (technical) | Lead (technical) |
|---|---|---|
| **Stance** | "I make my team's technical work excellent" | "I shape how engineering works org-wide" |
| **Scope** | One Roomle surface or a tight cluster (e.g., Rubens + Web SDK + embedding) | The Roomle ↔ Homag engineering seam, technical strategy for Roomle as a whole, and Roomle's position in the broader Homag Group / configurator industry |
| **Architecture** | Owns architecture in their area | Owns architecture across the organization; ensures system consistency |
| **Vision** | Drives technical direction within the team | Defines long-term technical vision (2-5 year horizon); anticipates industry shifts |
| **Decisions** | Makes significant technical decisions for the team | Influences company strategy; their perspective shapes investment and direction |
| **Mentoring** | Mentors engineers; grows team capability | Grows other technical leaders (Senior L2s); develops successors |
| **External presence** | Represents the team to stakeholders inside the company | Represents the company externally; recognized in the broader industry |
| **Innovation** | Researches and introduces new tech for the team | Creates novel approaches / patterns adopted internally or by the external community |

## Why this distinction matters

Both Senior L2 and Lead are technical roles, but operating at different organizational scales. The shift is not just "more impact" — it is **a different kind of work**, requiring cross-org influence, external presence, and long-horizon thinking that Senior L2 does not need.

This means:

- Lead is **structurally rare** in a 30-person company — there may only be 0-1 Leads at any time. Lead requires both demonstrated capability *and* an available org-shaping scope. The framework cannot promise everyone at Senior L2 a path to Lead; if the scope is not there, the level is not there. Compensation can continue to grow within Senior L2 in that case (see [Overview](../../00%20Overview.md#starting-level-progression)) — strong L2 work is rewarded through salary, not by manufacturing a Lead role that doesn't exist.
- Lead is the **top of the ladder**. Executive-leadership positions sit outside this ladder; see [Executive Leadership — Outside the Career Ladder](../../levels/99%20Executive%20Leadership.md).
- The "team → org" shift is real: a great Senior L2 architect for their team may lack the cross-org influence and external presence Leads need. Promotion to Lead should not be assumed automatic from doing the L2 job well — it requires demonstrating org-level behaviors.
- Time horizon differs sharply: Senior L2 thinks in quarters and projects; Lead thinks in years and platforms.
