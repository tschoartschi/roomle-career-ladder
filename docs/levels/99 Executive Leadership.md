# 99 Executive Leadership — Outside the Career Ladder

Executive-leadership positions (e.g., CTO, Head of Engineering, VP-level roles) sit **outside this career ladder** for three reasons:

- **They depend on structural opportunity, not just skill growth.** In a ~30-person company there are only 1–2 such positions. You cannot "ladder" into something that requires an organizational seat to be available.
- **They require active seeking and claiming.** An engineer aiming for an executive position must proactively seek the role and demonstrate readiness beyond the Lead level. It is not an automatic progression.
- **They require specific individual negotiations** regarding scope, responsibilities, and compensation that go beyond what a standardized career ladder can define.

Reaching **Lead (5a/5b)** is the highest level in this career ladder. From there, a move into executive leadership is a fundamentally different kind of career step — one driven by organizational need, personal initiative, and mutual agreement between the employee and the company.

For where this boundary becomes relevant in level transitions, see [Senior L2 → Lead (technical)](../background/boundaries/04-senior-l2-vs-lead-technical.md) and [Senior L2 → Lead (management)](../background/boundaries/05-senior-l2-vs-lead-management.md).
