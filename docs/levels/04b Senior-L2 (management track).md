# 04b Senior L2 (Management Track)

Management position → counterpart: [04a Senior-L2 (technical track)](04a%20Senior-L2%20(technical%20track).md)

People multiplier

> **Current organisational note.** At Roomle today there is no Senior L2 (management track) role filled \u2014 the responsibilities described here are currently covered by VP Engineering. This page describes the level as a *destination*: what someone moving into people management at Roomle would actually be accountable for, and what would have to be true for the role to exist as a distinct slot.

> A Senior L2 on the management track multiplies their impact through people. They are responsible for the team's delivery, growth, and well-being. At our current company scale, this role operates heavily as a **"Player-Coach"**, managing 1-4 engineers while still contributing directly. They use their strong technical judgment to make good decisions, support their team, and participate in discussions. Their primary accountability is the team's output and health, not just their own ticket delivery.
>
> This level is the threshold for **personelle Managementaufgaben** in the IT KV sense — ownership of team delivery, performance management, and people development — which is why it maps to ST2 rather than ST1.

_Boundaries: [Senior L1 → Senior L2](../background/boundaries/03-senior-l1-vs-l2.md) · [Senior L2 → Lead](../background/boundaries/05-senior-l2-vs-lead-management.md)._

## Technical Mastery
**tldr;** A Senior L2 (management) maintains strong technical credibility, uses technical judgment to guide their team's decisions, participates meaningfully in architectural discussions, and ensures technical quality without being the one implementing.

* **Technical credibility**: has enough depth and currency in the team's technology to earn the respect of the engineers they manage. Can participate in technical discussions without hand-waving. Has been a strong individual contributor before moving to this track.

* **Judgment, not implementation**: uses technical knowledge primarily for decision support — evaluating tradeoffs, spotting risks, asking the right questions — rather than for hands-on implementation. Knows when a proposed solution is over-engineered or under-designed.

* **Architectural awareness**: understands the team's systems well enough to participate in design reviews and architectural discussions. Can identify when decisions need more input or when the team is heading toward a dead end.

* **Capacity planning**: understands the technical complexity of the team's work well enough to plan capacity, assess estimates, and identify when the team is overcommitted, even for a very small team.

* **Hands-on balance (Player-Coach)**: depending on the size of their team (which may be just 1-3 people), they still write code actively (often up to 50% of the time). However, they avoid taking the critical path tasks that would block the team if management emergencies arise.

* **Quality oversight**: ensures the team maintains high technical standards — not by reviewing every PR, but by establishing practices (code review culture, testing standards) and trusting colleagues to uphold them.

## Learning
**tldr;** A Senior L2 (management) develops their team's skills, creates structured learning opportunities, and grows their own management capabilities alongside their technical knowledge.

* **Develops others**: identifies skill gaps in their team members and creates opportunities to close them — whether through stretch assignments, pairing, training, or mentoring connections.
* **Structured growth planning**: works with each team member on career development. Understands individual goals and creates paths toward them. Holds regular 1:1s focused on growth, not just status.
* **Creates learning opportunities**: establishes or maintains team-level learning practices — innovation days, tech talks, conference attendance, book clubs, pair programming rotations.
* **Grows as a manager**: actively invests in their own management skills — through reading, courses, peer groups, or mentorship from more experienced managers. Recognizes management as a craft that requires continuous development.
* **Learns from the team**: stays technically current by learning from the team's work — through design reviews, demos, and staying connected to the codebase. Doesn't become disconnected from the technical reality.

## Responsibility and Ownership
**tldr;** A Senior L2 (management) owns the team's delivery and health, empowers individuals through delegation and coaching, and is accountable for outcomes — both technical and human.

* **Owns team delivery**: is accountable for the team's output. While they may still do some of the work themselves as a Player-Coach, they ensure the structural conditions exist for the rest of the team to deliver consistently.
* **Empowers through delegation**: gives team members meaningful ownership over their work. Delegates real responsibility, not just tasks, even when it might be faster to just code it themselves.
* **Coaching over directing**: helps team members solve problems rather than solving problems for them. Pairs constantly but avoids taking over the keyboard instinctively.
* **Performance management**: has honest, timely conversations about performance. Addresses underperformance early and constructively. Celebrates and rewards strong performance.
* **Shields the team**: protects the team from unnecessary organizational noise, shifting priorities, and context switching — without isolating them from important business context.
* **Resource stewardship**: manages the team's time, energy, and focus as finite resources. Pushes back on overcommitment. Knows when to say no.
* **Accountable for people outcomes**: takes responsibility not just for what the team delivers, but for how people feel, whether they're growing, and whether the team is sustainable long-term.

## Collaboration and Communication
**tldr;** A Senior L2 (management) leads team dynamics, manages stakeholder relationships, communicates up and down effectively, and creates a healthy team culture.

* **Leads team dynamics**: shapes how the team works together — meetings, rituals, decision-making processes, conflict resolution. Creates psychological safety where people can speak up, disagree, and take risks.
* **Stakeholder management**: manages relationships with product, business, and other engineering teams. Translates between technical and non-technical stakeholders. Ensures the team's work is understood and valued.
* **Communicates up**: keeps leadership informed about the team's progress, risks, and needs. Doesn't surprise their own manager. Advocates for the team's priorities in planning and resourcing discussions.
* **Communicates down**: ensures the team understands the "why" behind priorities and decisions. Provides business context that helps engineers make better day-to-day decisions.
* **Facilitates alignment**: when the team disagrees on approach, facilitates productive discussion and drives toward decisions. Knows when to let the team decide and when to make the call themselves.
* **Cross-team collaboration**: works effectively with other team leads and managers. Identifies dependencies, coordinates shared work, and resolves conflicts before they escalate.
* **Hiring and onboarding**: participates actively in hiring — defining what the team needs, interviewing candidates, making hiring decisions. Ensures new team members are onboarded effectively.

## Influence
**tldr;** A Senior L2 (management) shapes team processes and culture, influences beyond their immediate team, and ensures the team's impact is visible to the organization.

* **Shapes team practices**: actively improves how the team works — processes, rituals, tools, team agreements. Drives changes based on feedback and observation, not just top-down mandates.
* **Beyond the team**: influences decisions and practices beyond their own team — contributing to engineering-wide standards, participating in cross-team planning, and sharing what works.
* **Team visibility**: ensures the team's work, achievements, and challenges are visible to the broader organization. Advocates for the team's contributions in company-wide forums.
* **Culture building**: deliberately shapes the team's culture — how people treat each other, how decisions get made, how failures are handled, how successes are celebrated.
* **Trusted manager**: the team trusts them with difficult topics — career concerns, interpersonal issues, disagreements. They've earned this trust through consistent, fair behavior.
* **Organizational citizenship**: contributes to the broader organization beyond their team — interviewing, mentoring people outside their team, participating in company initiatives.
* **Role model**: models the behavior they expect — transparency, accountability, work-life balance, curiosity, and respect. People learn from watching them as much as from what they say.
