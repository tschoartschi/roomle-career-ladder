# Axes

The career ladder evaluates every level across **five axes**. Each axis has its own per-level expectation; the verb names (*Applies / Solves / Designs / Masters / Creates*, etc.) are what the dots in the [[00 Overview#What the shape looks like by level|Overview shape table]] actually mean.

The axes are loosely inspired by [engineeringladders.com](https://www.engineeringladders.com/) — see [[engineeringladders|local summary]] for the original Technology / System / People / Process / Influence model — but adapted to Roomle's reality.

## Technical Mastery

Technical mastery is crucial for software engineers because it enables them to develop high-quality software that meets the needs of the user, and is scalable and maintainable. It also ensures that they can effectively communicate with team members, contribute to technical decision-making, and stay up-to-date with the latest industry developments. Ultimately, technical mastery allows a software engineer to deliver effective solutions and drive business success.

There are 5 stages of technical mastery (each stage includes the previous stage):

1. **Applies**: applies known patterns and tools to solve defined problems within their area.
2. **Solves**: solves standard problems independently within their domain.
3. **Designs**: designs solutions to complex problems; able to teach others.
4. **Masters**: masters the full stack/domain; drives technical decisions.
5. **Creates**: creates novel approaches and technologies adopted by others.

Subject-specific technical skills (e.g., the difference between a frontend and a backend engineer at the same level) are captured in the [[web-frontend|domain]] and [[ai-tooling|cross-cutting]] role profiles, not in this general axis.

## Learning

The ability to learn and grasp new things is important for a software engineer because the field of software development is constantly evolving, and being able to adapt to new technologies and techniques is essential to remain relevant and effective. Additionally, software engineers are often required to work on projects that involve unfamiliar domains, so being able to quickly understand and learn about these domains is crucial for success.

There are 5 stages of learning (each stage includes the previous stage):

1. **Adopts**: learns and adopts patterns that are presented.
2. **Grows**: gathers knowledge in their knowledge domain.
3. **Explores**: actively takes the initiative to learn new things, also outside of their knowledge domain.
4. **Educates**: is able to teach new insights to others.
5. **Evangelizes**: can get buy-in for new ideas learned.

## Responsibility and Ownership

Responsibility and ownership are crucial for a software engineer because software development involves complex systems and processes that require attention to detail, critical thinking, and problem-solving. Responsibility means being aware of the potential impact of one's work on end-users and stakeholders. Taking ownership of one's work means being accountable for its quality and timeliness. This mindset fosters a culture of trust, collaboration, and continuous improvement, leading to better software products.

1. **Acknowledges**: understands the importance of responsibility and ownership, but has not yet fully internalized it in their work.
2. **Owns**: takes responsibility for their work and accepts the consequences of their actions.
3. **Drives**: actively seeks out ways to improve their work and takes ownership of projects beyond their assigned tasks.
4. **Empowers**: is trusted to make decisions and is given autonomy to manage projects; empowers others to take ownership.
5. **Mentors**: serves as a role model and mentor to others, instilling a culture of responsibility and ownership within the team.

## Collaboration and Communication

Collaboration and communication are critical for a software engineer because software development is rarely a solitary effort. Collaborating with team members and stakeholders helps ensure that everyone is aligned on goals and expectations and that the end product meets the needs of the end users. Effective communication helps prevent misunderstandings, reduces errors, and fosters a culture of trust and teamwork, leading to better software products.

1. **Contributes**: participates in team efforts and communicates status of their own work.
2. **Collaborates**: actively collaborates with team members, communicating effectively and contributing to a positive team culture.
3. **Facilitates**: facilitates collaboration and communication within the team, ensuring that everyone is aligned on goals and expectations.
4. **Leads**: takes on leadership roles within the team, moderating discussions and driving alignment across stakeholders.
5. **Advocates**: serves as an advocate for collaboration and communication within the organization, promoting best practices and mentoring others.

## Influence

Influence inside an organization is important for software engineers because it enables them to drive positive change and contribute to the success of the organization. By building strong relationships, demonstrating expertise, and communicating effectively, a software engineer can influence decision-making and shape the direction of projects and initiatives. This can lead to greater job satisfaction, career advancement opportunities, and ultimately, better software products and outcomes for the organization.

1. **Observes**: is learning how the organization works and building initial relationships.
2. **Contributes**: actively contributes to projects and initiatives, building credibility within the team.
3. **Influences**: has established expertise and credibility within the organization and is able to influence decision-making.
4. **Leads**: inspires and guides others to achieve common goals; influences direction beyond own team.
5. **Shapes**: shapes the long-term vision and strategy of the organization at a high level.
