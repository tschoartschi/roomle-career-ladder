# 03 Senior L1

Technical position → no management counterpart

Strong contributor

> A Senior L1 is a strong individual contributor who drives quality, standards, and initiative within their team. They design complex systems, make sound tradeoffs, explore new technologies proactively, and take full ownership of their projects through the entire lifecycle. They facilitate collaboration and influence decision-making at the team level — but their impact is through personal excellence and team-level leadership, not yet through managing others or driving cross-team initiatives.

## Technical Mastery
**tldr;** A senior software engineer is capable of designing complex systems, making tradeoffs, automating tedious tasks, is self-organized, can maintain an existing code base and assess risks, pays attention to details, has high standards for excellence and is acting professionally

* **Software Design & Architecture**: can design complex systems from scratch, can lead a discussion about Software Design & Architecture, can bring those discussions to a conclusion, can analyze complex problems, and come up with creative solutions to solve them. Recognizing patterns from previous work and is able to pick them up and use them. Is independent and a self-learner. Perceives deviations from normal patterns.

* **Making tradeoffs**: knows when something is good enough, can balance between technical excellence and "getting shit done", knows the Pareto Principle (80/20 rule) and knows when it's time to focus on the 80% and when we should tackle the remaining 20%. Knows how to document those tradeoffs so that everyone in the organization is aware of them.

* **Automatization**: identifies tedious recurring tasks and knows how to automate them. Can share the automatization with the whole team to free up everyone's time, making the whole team more productive

* **Demonstrates technical mastery** in their area of expertise: for details see the section "Subject-Specific Technical Skills". But some generic skills are: is a frequent contributor to "hard to fix" bugs. Points out problems in PRs. Can define guidelines that help the whole team produce higher-quality code. Identifies common problems (in PRs etc) and suggests standards that help to remove those common problems.

* **Self-organization**: Scopes and stages work into well-defined milestones to avoid a monolithic deliverable, knows when to do the work themselves and when to ask others to pick it up, knows when it's time to ask and knows when it's time to do your own research (dyor). 

* **High standards** for engineering excellence: Attention to detail, following best practices and helping to evolve them. Solid understanding of security threats, optimize performance (knows the dangers of premature optimizations), writes unit tests that ensure code quality

* **Knowledge**: very proficient in their area of expertise (see: "Subject-Specific Technical Skills") but also about the broader current technology landscape. Is the go-to person for at least one technology and takes the initiative to learn new ones. Has a deep understanding of the domain in which they work.

* **Debugging / Bug fixing**: can distill complex problems, can independently solve different issues due to past experience. Is able to find the root cause of an issue, and knows the difference between "fixing a problem" and just "patching" a problem. Is able to debug things like race conditions, deadlocks, etc that do not happen if you add break-points. Often helps and assists others in debugging sessions. Can debug and understand code that they didn't write. While debugging they develop a broad understanding of the code, even if they didn't write it. Knows when to apply a quick patch and when to do a "real" fix. Knows what to do when a "real" fix would explode in scope. Only uses trial and error as an escape hatch, and never fixes a problem with trial and error without understanding the deeper underlying problem. Knows during a debugging session when to take a step back and start again from scratch. Does not use a "mit dem Kopf durch die Wand" approach (does not go into the rabbit hole).

* **Professionalism**: Act in a professional and ethical manner at all times, including being dependable, accountable, and respectful to others. Knows that it is important to use flawless and thoughtful language, especially in written form. Almost/never makes the same mistake twice. Does not create unnecessary friction e.g.: PRs are polished before submitted, no typos, communicates actively when discussions go out of hand. Many PRs can be merged quickly because most of the time there are only a few small remarks from the reviewer.

* **Maintenance**: knows how to reduce technical debt (knows what type of technical debt to remove on the go, for which we need to block time and which requires a feature freeze, and when to start a rewrite from scratch. Similar to what [Shopify suggests](https://shopify.engineering/technical-debt-25-percent-rule)). Is able to identify and refactor code smells. Knows how to improve code structure and architecture of existing software. Identifies and proactively tackles technical debt before it grows into unmanageable debt.

* **Risk Management**: can identify risks. Either risk due to new technical developments (e.g.: some encryption is broken or some exploit aka "log4j vulnerability" was found), risk due to rewrites, risk due to bugs etc.  Knows when a change to the code needs consultation. Knows the fine line between "fixing code" and "rewriting code", and knows the risks of rewriting.

## Subject-Specific Technical Skills

### Web frontend

**tldr;** A senior frontend engineer should be able to teach HTML, CSS, and advanced JavaScript to junior developers, should have strong UX/UI design knowledge, is able to communicate effectively with designers and cross-functional teams, stays up-to-date with frameworks and libraries, prioritizes user experience, understands design systems and browser compatibility, and possess full-stack skills.

* **Teaching fundamentals**: can teach HTML, CSS, and advanced JavaScript to Junior Developers
* **Design and UX/UI**: has deep knowledge of UX/UI patterns, has a very good "eye" for visual glitches, knows the current trends of design
* **Collaboration with designers**: can easily communicate with designers, can bridge the gap between "designer language" and "engineering language", can lead the discussions with designers so that it is ensured that the final product meets both design and technical requirements, helps designers to understand the technical impact of certain designs/layouts
* **Extremely strong communicator**: a web frontend engineer needs to be a very strong communicator because often web projects involve very cross-functional teams. Since the UI is often customer-facing a web frontend engineer needs to be able to communicate with salespeople, marketing people, designers but also other engineers.
* **Frameworks and libraries**: is up-to-date with the latest developments in the frameworks we use but is also knowledgeable about the broader JavaScript ecosystem. Knows what framework fatigue is and knows why going for the next hype is not always the best idea. Has a deep understanding of the frameworks in use, architectural, design-pattern-wise, and therefore knows the strength and weaknesses of those frameworks. Is proficient with the APIs of the framework and can use them in a way that is readable for non-framework experts while still utilizing its convenient features. Knows what pros/cons an external dependency has and knows how to keep all the dependencies up-to-date. Knows when it is a good time to upgrade. Can evaluate frameworks and make a decision on when to use which framework. Knows how to balance the framework's convenience with flexibility.
* **User experience**: has a deep understanding of UX and can apply current standards while coding without the need to always ask designers, knows when to apply performance optimizations that help UX, knows when it's better to improve perceived performance than actual performance, knows the target audience and knows what this audience needs. Understands the mobile landscape and knows what are the key differences in UX on desktop and on mobile
* **Design systems**: has an understanding of Design Systems, can spread the idea of a Design System to the team, knows when to use Design Systems, can take Design Systems into place, and is able to maintain Design Systems.
* **Browser compatibility**: knows how to prevent cross-browser compatibility issues, can make a good tradeoff between DX and backward compatibility
* **Full-stack skills**: has shown that they have full-stack skills, either already in production apps, side projects, at Innovation Days, or in integration examples. This should be hands-on knowledge not only theoretical knowledge. 

## Learning
**tldr;** A senior software engineer has a strong passion for continuous learning, a growth mindset that challenges the status quo, demonstrates learning agility, and is proactively seeking new opportunities for professional development.

* **Passion for learning**: has a strong passion for learning, has multiple sources to stay up-to-date (books, community, meetups, conferences, newsletters, podcasts, blogs, Twitter, youtube, mastodon...), shares the knowledge within the team, gets hands-on experience on new tech (Innovations Days, side projects, customer integration projects etc). Learns through engagement (in the company, or tech community, or at meetups/conferences etc).
* **Growth mindset**: always challenges the status-quo, the own status-quo but also the status-quo of the organization
* **Learning agility**: demonstrates the commitment to continuous learning and professional development. Always tries to understand new technologies, tools, and methodologies. Is proactive in seeking out new opportunities to learn and grow.


## Responsibility and Ownership
**tldr;** A senior software engineer independently drives their own projects through the full lifecycle, takes ownership and accountability for their work, delivers reliably, proactively improves their area, and takes initiative to solve problems beyond assigned tasks.

* **Full lifecycle ownership**: independently responsible for the entire lifecycle of their own projects including engineering design, development, deployment, and operation
* **Trusted with autonomy**: is trusted to be set aside to work on technical topics they identified (e.g.: upgrade, improve testing, remove impediments etc)
* **Gets things done**: has the ownership and responsibility in their team to push their tasks to an end. Can "get shit done"
* **Solo capability**: the ability to be a sole developer for a certain amount of time and still not lose track or get lost
* **Accountable**: holds themselves accountable. Can pick up previous work and build on top of it without complaining about what was done in the past.
* **Research and presentation**: can do research and present results to a larger audience; the results are the basis for developing new features or changing the current architecture
* **Reliable delivery**: regularly delivers on time and is constantly working to make accurate estimates and deliver on those estimates
* **Proactive problem-solving**: takes initiative to identify and solve important problems within their area. Is able to coordinate with others if it's a cross-cutting problem.
* **Technical ownership**: owns the technical-, testing- and performance parts for their projects
* **Process improvement**: shows initiative and independently executes process improvements for the pod
* **Intrinsic motivation**: intrinsically cares about projects they work on, knows how to present those projects, knows how to explain why those projects deliver value
* **Defends quality**: has the courage to defend and explain spill overs. Knows when a spill over makes more sense than a quick hacky solution

## Collaboration and Communication
**tldr;** A senior software engineer understands business needs, communicates effectively with diverse stakeholders, actively facilitates team discussions, and supports others through feedback and knowledge sharing.

* **Business awareness**: understands business and product domain well: also knows what's happening outside of the Software Engineering department
* **Clear communication**: has the ability to communicate clearly with technical and non-technical people. Makes sure their message comes clear regardless of the audience. Can communicate effectively with all stakeholders, including business analysts, project managers, designers, and other engineers
* **Hunting for business knowledge**: actively searches how the product works, how users interact with it, why it works as it does etc.
* **Communicates ideas proactively**: is not simply "the coder in the corner". Actively shares opinions and proposals within the team. Takes the initiative to suggest team-wide guidelines (code-wise, process-wise, etc).
* **Constructive feedback**: collaborates effectively with team members, and is able to give and receive feedback constructively.
* **Organizing**: takes the initiative to bring together the right people to discuss specific topics. Knows when to escalate problems that have a wider scope.
* **Supporting others**: supports colleagues through code reviews, documentation, and technical guidance. Helps others grow by sharing knowledge and providing constructive feedback.
* **General**: A communicative collaborator, not a passive bystander. Unambiguous communication that's to the point, assumes good intentions. Humility — knows that it's impossible to know everything and there will always be someone smarter in some aspect.
* **Assistance to PO**: can help the PO and help to refine User Stories

## Influence
**tldr;** A senior software engineer has established credibility within the team, delivers impactful results, provides valuable PR reviews, challenges team processes, and influences decision-making within their team.

* **Established credibility**: has established credibility and influence within the team through consistent delivery and expertise
* **PR reviews as influence**: creates influence through PR reviews: by giving valuable advice in the reviews, new standards and guidelines can emerge
* **Impactful results**: is focusing on delivering impactful results: knows which initiatives bring the bigger value at which time. Knows how to market maintenance work and why maintenance is impactful.
* **Go-to expert**: go-to expert in at least one area of the codebase
* **Decision influence**: influences decision-making at the team level on technical matters
* **Team engagement**: shows engagement within their team and contributes to team-wide discussions
* **Reliability**: people know they are reliable and professional.
* **Ambition**: has an appreciation for their own work and is ambitious about creating great solutions
* **Challenges processes**: challenges the team processes, looking for ways to improve them
* **Team-wide impact**: makes an impact on their entire team, not just on their direct co-workers
* **Positive attitude**: maintains a positive attitude towards challenges and setbacks, and demonstrates resilience in the face of difficulties.
