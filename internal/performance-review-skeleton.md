# Performance Review — Skeleton (Pre-HR-Alignment Draft)

> **Status: DRAFT. Not operational.** This is a pre-HR-alignment proposal for what the regular performance review document should contain — both to satisfy the career ladder framework and to handle the § 15 II IT-KV obligations the framework currently has no operational backing for.
>
> The goal is to enter the HR conversation with a concrete shape to react to, not to commit to this exact structure. Once HR has reviewed, this skeleton either moves to `docs/` (operationalised) or gets revised here based on their input.

## Why this exists

The ladder defines competencies (level pages, axes, role profiles) and decision points (boundary docs, L2 entry criteria). What it does *not* define is the regular operational rhythm in which engineers and managers actually use the framework: when do reviews happen, what do they cover, how are they documented, what do they trigger?

This skeleton proposes that operational rhythm. The same artifact also doubles as the legal evidence trail for § 15 II ¶ 2 (the 36-month assessment), § 15 I (8) (Dienstzettel updates), and any Verwarnung/improvement process if performance becomes an issue.

## Cadence

- **Universal annual review** for every engineer, regardless of level. Triggered by calendar, not by performance suspicion.
- **Additional "level fit" conversation** 6–12 months before any KV Vorrückungsstufe step the employee is approaching. Layered on top of the annual review, not a replacement.
- **Mid-year light-touch check-in** (optional, manager discretion) for engineers on a growth plan toward the next level.
- All reviews are triggered by calendar events (employee anniversary, KV clock, growth-plan check-in date), never by *"we think there is a performance problem"*. Performance concerns surface inside the review structure, not as separate ad-hoc reviews.

The universal calendar-driven cadence is deliberate. It means employees never receive a "review" out of nowhere; the discussion is always part of the normal rhythm.

## Document sections

### 1. Current performance assessment

- Snapshot of the engineer's shape on the five ladder axes (Technical Mastery, Learning, Responsibility & Ownership, Collaboration & Communication, Influence).
- Reference verb per axis at the current level (per the [Overview Stage Verbs table](../docs/00%20Overview.md#stage-verbs-by-level)).
- For each axis: brief evidence summary — concrete examples from the last review period.
- Cross-reference any applicable role profile (domain) and the AI Tooling cross-cutting profile.
- Notes on any axis where the engineer is operating above or below the current level (the spiky reality acknowledged in the Overview).

### 2. KV classification fit

- **Current KV Vorrückungsstufe** and the date the current band took effect.
- **Predominant work content assessment.** Does the work the employee is actually performing match the current KV band's description (§ 15 II IT-KV)?
- **For employees approaching or past 36 months of relevant ST1/ST2 experience (incl. anrechenbare Vordienstzeiten):** a written assessment under § 15 II ¶ 2 IT-KV of whether the predominant work matches the ST2 description. Signed and dated. **This section IS the § 15 II ¶ 2 assessment — no separate artifact required.**
- If reclassification is indicated: action assigned, target date, Dienstzettel update flagged (see section 6).

### 3. Vordienstzeit reconciliation

- Confirm that prior-employment evidence on file for the employee is complete and current.
- Note any corrections discovered since the last review (e.g., previously uncounted prior IT employment surfaced).
- If the KV Vorrückungsstufe clock requires adjustment based on the reconciliation, record it here and trigger the Dienstzettel update (section 6).

### 4. Development plan

- Concrete development goals for the next 6–12 months.
- If a **growth plan toward the next level** is in place (per the Overview "Growth plan instead of provisional promotion" mechanism): record check-in date, gap-closure criteria, and what evidence will be considered at the check-in.
- Support offered: training budget, pairing arrangements, conference attendance, mentorship pairings, time allocation for learning.
- Any axes where the engineer is operating below the current level: explicit development plan for closing the gap.

### 5. Compensation review

- Current salary vs KV floor for the current Vorrückungsstufe.
- Current salary vs internal salary band for the role/title (once those bands are formally defined — see the open follow-up on KV bands → internal salary bands).
- Discussion of above-floor compensation per the Overview *"Compensation growth is possible at Senior L1 without moving to Senior L2"* mechanism, where applicable.
- Outcome: recommended salary action (no change, adjustment to KV floor, adjustment to internal band, deep-expert above-floor adjustment). Effective date.

### 6. Dienstzettel implications

- Does anything in this review change classification, role, or work content sufficient to trigger a § 15 I (8) IT-KV Dienstzettel update?
- If yes: action assigned, target date, who files the update where.
- This section exists so the Dienstzettel update is never overlooked when a review produces a change.

## Sign-off

- **Engineer signature + date** — acknowledgement of receipt and discussion, not necessarily agreement.
- **Manager signature + date** — completion of the review.
- **HR file location** — where this document is filed for retention.
- **Next review trigger** — date and reason (annual cycle, KV step approaching, growth-plan check-in).

## Variants

Two variants of this template are likely needed:

1. **Standard annual review.** All six sections, light-touch where nothing has changed.
2. **§ 15 II ¶ 2 assessment review.** Same template, but section 2 is mandatory in writing (not optional brief notes) and triggers an Umreihung decision. Applies at the 36-month mark of relevant experience and on every subsequent review until the employee is at ST2 or has been written-out of ST2 eligibility based on actual work content.

Both variants are the same document, just with different emphasis in section 2.

## What this skeleton deliberately does *not* include

- **Numeric scoring.** No 1–5 ratings per axis. The framework already uses level verbs (*Applies / Solves / Designs / Masters / Creates*) — those are the rating. Numeric scoring on top of qualitative verbs would invite false precision and Gleichbehandlung challenges on whether the numbers were applied consistently.
- **Forced ranking or distributions.** No "only 20% can be 'exceeds expectations'" mechanic. Forced distributions are a known Gleichbehandlung risk.
- **360-style peer review machinery.** The 30-person org is too small to make peer review anonymous *and* meaningful. The skeleton assumes manager + engineer + optional second evaluator from leadership, not a multi-rater workflow.
- **OKRs or quantitative goal tracking.** The skeleton focuses on level fit and KV obligations, not goal achievement. Goal tracking can sit alongside this review document but is a separate concern.

These omissions are intentional. If HR considers any of them necessary, that's the conversation to have when reviewing this skeleton — but the default is *less machinery is better at this org size*.

## Open questions for the HR conversation

- Should the § 15 II ¶ 2 36-month assessment use this document, or be a separate sibling artifact? *Current proposal: same document, section 2.*
- Should the Senior L1 → Senior L2 promotion decision use this document, or be a separate sibling artifact? *Current proposal: same document, with growth plan in section 4 and the explicit promotion outcome in section 5 + 6.*
- What is the retention period for review documents? *Standard Austrian employment record retention is 7 years post-employment for KV-relevant documents; HR to confirm.*
- Who has access to the filed documents (manager, HR, employee on request)?
- How does Betriebsrat involvement (if a Betriebsrat exists or is later elected) interact with the review process under § 99 ArbVG?
- Is a structured "intervention track" needed in addition to this template, or does the Performance-based Intervention Procedure (see follow-ups) chain from this document when section 1 surfaces sustained underperformance?

These questions are open by design and should be resolved in the HR conversation.
